Where the models go

These three tools ran on my cheap laptop. No graphics processing unit (GPU), a generation takes minutes, and the larger model costs about three times the smaller one. That makes where you put a model — and whether you put one there at all — an engineering decision rather than a taste.

gigi

I started with the conventional shape. gigi embeds documents and questions with a small multilingual encoder, retrieves by cosine similarity with maximal marginal relevance (MMR) for diversity, grades the candidates, and asks a local model to write one answer. A cross-encoder reranker sat in the middle because that is what you do.

A 288-configuration grid over the fixture corpus said otherwise. Reranking lowered mean reciprocal rank from 1.000 to 0.644 and answer basis from 1.000 to 0.604. On the composite score the paired bootstrap put the difference at [+0.065, +0.206] with p = 1.00 in favour of leaving it out. I removed the component; the encoder stayed. A model earned its place by being deleted.

acciughe

acciughe began as a product file, not a repository. The hypothesis there was that a knowledge graph — entities, relations, clusters — could answer the questions a folder of text cannot, those spanning notes written years apart, and describe the corpus too. It was marked tentative and expected to be revised. What shipped was a co-occurrence graph built from stopwords, a bounded walk over it, and one model call at the end to phrase the answer. The model does not choose which note to read; the graph does, and that is why the structure can be inspected.

When the walk cannot support a claim, the command refuses rather than writing one. The summary became a count of the words more than one note holds: a sentence about a whole corpus has no citations, and this was a tool whose one rule was that every claim points at a note. A guard checks the quotes behind an answer; it catches fabrication, not relevance.

The product file stayed where it was written and eventually stopped describing the code; the tests became the specification. On the next project I kept the document current as measurements arrived.

grappoli

grappoli is the third attempt at the same problem — a branch too large to read — with no question to answer. It produces a map: regions, what each one is about, and how they touch.

The map is pure arithmetic. Embeddings, cosine similarity, a k-nearest-neighbour graph, community detection. No model call builds it. I kept term frequency–inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) alongside the encoder as a control, and the encoder earned its keep: adjusted Rand index (ARI) 0.904 and 1.000 on two fixtures against TF-IDF’s 0.435 and 0.494. One model wrote the region descriptions, one call per region, prompted with that region’s notes only.

The two generators needed telling apart. Asked to judge their descriptions, the smaller model flagged ten claims, every one a sentence lying verbatim in the notes. A cross-encoder trained on entailment, decoupled from the model it judged, scored them level on average — 0.762 against 0.772. The relation step separated them: asked one pair at a time, the smaller model wrote about one region where a relation between two belonged; the larger one wrote the relation, at about three times the generation cost.

Choosing the work was the other experiment. A conversation where the model chose its tools returned no relations in six cells; naming the pair and asking one local question per call returned a valid relation in all eighteen. I bought relations that exist and gave up the model’s choice.

What the three share

Language was the other constant. gigi moved to a multilingual encoder and a golden set in four languages. acciughe tried deriving stopwords from corpus frequencies rather than keeping lists; the machinery grew and the result did not justify it. grappoli inherited the lists. Its mixed-language fixture is the edge of what has been scored: adjusted Rand index −0.044, with no setting above 0.212, the encoder grouping by language where the reference groups by topic. That is a limit of the record, not a result about the corpus the tool was built for.

Across the three, every model instance is an encoder, a generator or a judge, and each has a measured reason for sitting where it does — including the ones I took out. The reranker, the whole-map conversation and the self-judge each gave way to simpler parts that could be checked. Each project keeps a file of what is still undecided and the measurement that would settle it. That file, more than the code, is where the decisions were made.
